02.A Profile, in his own words

An audience
with the Comte.

Le Comte de Whiskersworth III was born in a sunbeam on a stack of Architectural Digest, May 1991. He has never worked a day in his life. He has, however, developed strong opinions about cat food, and we have agreed to publish them.

He insists on real meat. He refuses anything described as a “by-product.” He will not eat from a can — the opener offends him personally. The snap-pack was his idea. The pricing was ours.

He is not for sale. The paté is. Adjacent.

03.The Snap-Pack

One tray.
One foil. One small woman in Lyon.

  1. I.

    Peel.

    A small foil pull-tab. The kind that makes a sound. Cats hear this sound from three rooms away.

  2. II.

    Serve.

    Into a small dish, or, if you must, present it on the tray with both hands and a small bow.

  3. III.

    Witness gratitude.

    Or don't. Cats are not contractually obligated to thank you. Smug eye contact is statistically likely.

Recyclable tray. Biodegradable foil. The same five real ingredients we started with, in the same kitchen, by the same exhausted woman.

05.A Day in His Life

Champagne wishes,
and Caté paté dreams.

He rises at 4:13 AM, which is, of course, your problem. He inspects the kitchen. He inspects you. He returns to the chaise. By 4:14 he expects breakfast — on the silver tray, peeled, plated, garnished with a sprig of dill he will not eat.

— In the voice of the late, great voiceover man.

06.Ingredients

And now — the actual food.
