Meet the Spokescat

The brand's only employee, and easily the worst-paid.

Le Comte de Whiskersworth III was born in a sunbeam on a stack of Architectural Digest, May 1991. He has never worked a day in his life. He has, however, developed strong opinions about cat food, and we have agreed to publish them.

He insists on real meat. He refuses anything described as a “by-product.” He will not eat from a can — the tin opener offends him personally. The snap-pack was his idea. The pricing was ours.

He is not for sale. The paté is. Adjacent.

The Snap-Pack

One tray. One foil. One small woman in Lyon.

We retired the can. It was loud, it had a sharp lid, and Le Comte refused to look at it. The snap-pack is quieter, prettier, and peels.

i.

Peel.

A small foil pull-tab. The kind that makes a satisfying sound. Cats hear this sound from three rooms away.

ii.

Serve.

Slide the paté into a small dish, or, if your cat is Le Comte, present it directly on the tray with both hands and a small bow.

iii.

Witness gratitude.

Or don't. Cats are not contractually obligated to say thank you. (Reaction not guaranteed. Smug eye contact, however, is statistically likely.)
